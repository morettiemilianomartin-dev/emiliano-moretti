**When I built my website I wanted to see whether I could do it all on free plans, and the domain ended up being the only thing I pay for.** I made the site with Claude, GitHub and Vercel, and I wrote about it in [how I built my personal website](/en/blog/personal-website-with-claude-github-vercel/). For the first few weeks it lived at emiliano-moretti.vercel.app, and today it's at emilianomoretti.com.

## Why didn't I stay on .vercel.app?

Technically there was no need to change anything. The website loaded just as fast and Google was already indexing it.

What bothered me was how the link looked. When I shared an article on LinkedIn, emiliano-moretti.vercel.app showed up underneath, which reads like a test project set up in someone else's house. I've been writing about trust and about how [the brand works before the first call](/en/blog/visual-identity-and-trust/), and my website's address is also part of that first impression.

## When is it worth buying?

Before sharing your first link. I bought it with the website already live and several posts out there, and that cost me some work.

I had to make the old address redirect to the new one, tell Google about the change from Search Console and register the domain in Bing. Then I changed the link on LinkedIn, X and GitHub. The posts I had already published still show the old address in the preview; they work because they redirect, but they stayed like that.

None of those tasks is hard. There are several of them, and you avoid them if the domain is there from the start.

## What does it have to do with SEO?

What Google and Bing learn about a website, which pages it has and who links to it, stays tied to its address. With my own domain, that history is mine. If I leave Vercel tomorrow, I take the address and everything that has built up on it with me.

## And GEO?

GEO is the same idea, thought out for AI assistants: when someone asks ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity about a topic, you want the answer to use your website as a source. More and more people search that way, and I'm interested in learning how to be there.

I built some things into the website from the start. Each article opens with a short summary, has its headings written as questions and ends with frequently asked questions with direct answers. There's also an llms.txt file that summarizes the site for language models, structured data that explains who I am and what each page is about, and an automatic notice to Bing every time I publish an article.

I don't know yet whether any AI is citing me. The website is only a few weeks old and it's too early to measure.

## Why does it interest me so much?

I recently wrote about [Pibe Valderrama's fake haircut](/en/blog/pibe-valderrama-fake-haircut/) and how the media covered that campaign without charging. An AI naming you when someone asks about a topic is pretty similar: it's a mention you didn't pay for, and it comes because what you published is useful for answering. If that ever happens, I want the link that shows up to be one that belongs to me.
