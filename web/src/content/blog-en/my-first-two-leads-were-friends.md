**I had just published my website and two people had already reached out asking for help with a sales flow like mine. Both are close friends, and both messaged me on WhatsApp.** I expected a comment or two, a "nice one, congrats". I didn't expect concrete requests so quickly.

I'd sent the site to the first one before publishing it, so he could tell me whether the story came across. He gave me his opinion and also asked me to help him build something similar. The second one saw the post on LinkedIn, liked it, and a second later messaged me on WhatsApp.

Both made me think about something I wrote on this same blog about [what I delegate to AI and what I don't](/en/blog/what-i-delegate-to-ai-and-what-i-dont/).

## Why did they choose me?

There are surely people who can build far more sophisticated flows than mine: connecting more tools, coding custom integrations, automating steps I still do by hand. I come from sales, and I built [my process](/en/#como) with judgment and with Claude, without years of engineering behind it.

They chose me because they know me. They know how I work, they know that if I say something I deliver it, and they know I won't sell them something they don't need. That trust existed before the website.

## What can't AI copy?

AI can copy a process. It can research a lead, summarize a call, build a proposal and update the CRM. It already does all of that in my daily work, and does it well.

What it can't copy is trust, which is built over years of conversations, favors and doing what you said you'd do. It's the human component of selling, and it can't be transferred.

Today anyone can access the same tools I use, but the history you have with each person can't be downloaded.

## So what's the point of a personal brand?

I asked myself that. If the people who wrote already knew me, did the website do anything? I think it did, and it's the most interesting thing about this week.

My friends already trusted me, but they didn't know I worked this way. Look at the path the second one took: LinkedIn, a like and a WhatsApp message. The post is how he found out, but he wrote because he already trusted me. The website gave them a concrete reason to reach out: it showed them something useful, made by someone they already trusted.

That changes how I think about what I publish. I write so that people who already know me, and people who don't yet, can see what I'm up to and say "this interests me, let's talk". And if someone searching for "automate sales with AI" finds me along the way, welcome.

## What changes in how you sell with AI?

The same thing I keep saying, now with an example of my own. AI makes the work faster: I get to every conversation with context and without wasting time on data entry. But without trust there's no conversation to prepare for, which is why the time AI frees up has to go back to people. As I like to say, AI puts us on steroids, but trust is still the muscle.

## What do I take away?

It surprised me, in a good way. I thought the most valuable thing about this website would be showing how I work, and it ended up reminding me why people choose to work with someone.

To my two friends: thanks for being the first. And if you're reading this and also bringing AI into the way you work, [get in touch and let's compare notes](/en/#contacto).
